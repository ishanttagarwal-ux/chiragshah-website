Property & Real Estate

Litigation, conveyance, redevelopment & transfers

What is conveyance and do I need a lawyer for it?
+
Conveyance is the legal process of transferring property ownership via a Deed of Conveyance. A lawyer is strongly advisable as errors in drafting can lead to disputes or invalid transfers. We handle title search, drafting, stamp duty calculation, registration, and mutation — end to end.
What is a property litigation case?
+
Property litigation covers ownership disputes, partition suits, encroachment matters, tenancy conflicts, and disputes arising from sale agreements. We represent clients at all stages from legal notices to full court proceedings and appeals at the High Court.
What does your real estate and property transfer service cover?
+
Our services include title verification, drafting of sale deeds and agreements, stamp duty guidance, registration of documents, liaison with the Sub-Registrar's office, mutation of records, and handling any legal objections or disputes during the transfer process.
How can you help with housing society redevelopment?
+
We advise housing societies and individual members on redevelopment agreements, negotiating terms with developers, drafting Development Agreements, handling disputes with developers or other members, and ensuring compliance with RERA and Maharashtra Society laws throughout the redevelopment process.
What is the Rent Control Act and who does it apply to?
+
The Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 governs rental relationships for most older residential and commercial premises in Maharashtra. We handle eviction proceedings, fixation of standard rent, recovery of possession, and disputes related to illegal sub-letting or unlawful occupation.
Can you help if a builder has not delivered my flat on time?
+
Yes. Under RERA, developers are legally bound to deliver possession by the committed date. Buyers can claim compensation for delays. We file complaints before MahaRERA, negotiate with developers, and pursue legal remedies for delayed possession, defective construction, or failure to hand over promised amenities.

Family & Divorce

Divorce, custody, maintenance & matrimonial matters

What are the grounds for divorce under Indian law?
+
Under the Hindu Marriage Act, grounds include cruelty, adultery, desertion (2+ years), conversion of religion, mental disorder, communicable disease, and irretrievable breakdown (for mutual consent divorce). Personal laws for other religions have their own provisions. We advise clients on which grounds apply and the best approach for their situation.
How long does a divorce take in India?
+
Mutual consent divorce typically takes 6–18 months, including the 6-month cooling-off period (which courts may waive). Contested divorces can take 2–7 years or more depending on complexity and court schedules. We work efficiently and explore mediation wherever appropriate to reduce timelines.
Can I get maintenance or alimony during the divorce process?
+
Yes. Interim maintenance (pendente lite) can be claimed during the pendency of the case under Section 24 of the Hindu Marriage Act or similar provisions. Permanent alimony is determined at the time of final divorce. We assess entitlement and file the appropriate applications on your behalf.
How is child custody decided in India?
+
Courts determine custody primarily based on the best interests of the child — considering age, the child's preference (for older children), each parent's ability to provide, existing bonding, and stability of home environment. Both physical and joint custody arrangements are possible. We represent parents in custody proceedings and assist in mediated agreements.
What is a domestic violence case and how can you help?
+
The Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005 provides civil remedies including Protection Orders, Residence Orders, and Monetary Relief. We assist survivors in filing applications before the Magistrate's Court and obtaining emergency interim relief. We also handle related criminal complaints under Section 498A of the IPC if applicable.

Criminal Law

FIR, bail, trial defence & criminal proceedings

What should I do if an FIR has been filed against me?
+
Contact a lawyer immediately. Do not speak to police or make any statements without legal advice. Your lawyer can review the FIR, advise on anticipatory bail if you have not yet been arrested, challenge the FIR if it is frivolous or malicious, and represent you through the investigation and trial process.
What is anticipatory bail and how do I apply for it?
+
Anticipatory bail (Section 438 CrPC) is a pre-arrest bail granted when there is a reasonable apprehension of arrest. It is filed before the Sessions Court or High Court. We file such applications urgently, represent clients at hearings, and obtain protection before any arrest is made — critical in cases involving false or motivated complaints.
Can you help if I have been wrongly accused?
+
Yes. We regularly handle cases where clients are falsely implicated. Our approach includes gathering evidence of innocence, filing petitions to quash the FIR before the High Court under Section 482 CrPC, and vigorously contesting the prosecution's case at trial. We also advise on filing complaints against those who lodge false cases.
What is the legal process after arrest?
+
After arrest, the accused must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours. Police may seek remand, while the defence can apply for bail at the Magistrate, Sessions Court, or High Court level. A charge sheet is typically filed within 60–90 days, after which the trial commences. We represent clients at every stage from arrest to acquittal.
Do you handle cheque bounce (Section 138 NI Act) cases?
+
Yes. Dishonour of cheques under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act is a criminal offence carrying imprisonment up to 2 years or fine or both. We represent both complainants seeking recovery and accused persons defending prosecution, handling demand notices, filing of complaints, trial, and appeals.

Corporate & Company Law

Company disputes, cooperative societies & money recovery

What is company litigation and when do I need it?
+
Company litigation covers shareholder disputes, oppression and mismanagement petitions under the Companies Act, disputes over board decisions, winding-up petitions, and enforcement of shareholder agreements. You need legal assistance when governance disputes arise, minority shareholder rights are being suppressed, or company operations are conducted fraudulently.
Can you help with cooperative society disputes?
+
Yes. We handle all aspects of cooperative society law including election disputes, membership and share transfer issues, challenges to managing committee decisions, disputes between members, maintenance and account matters, and redevelopment disputes. We appear before the Cooperative Court, Registrar, and Bombay High Court.
What is a money recovery suit and how does it work?
+
A money recovery suit is filed to recover unpaid debts, loans, dues, security deposits, or damages from individuals or companies. We also handle recovery through arbitration, Debt Recovery Tribunals (DRT), and SARFAESI proceedings for secured creditors. We issue legal notices, file summary suits, and pursue enforcement of decrees.
Do you handle arbitration matters?
+
Yes. We have extensive experience in domestic arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996. We draft arbitration clauses, represent parties in proceedings, file and oppose applications to set aside awards, and assist with enforcement of arbitral awards. Arbitration is often faster and more confidential than court proceedings — we advise on whether it is the right forum.
Can you help register a company or LLP?
+
Yes. We assist with registration of Private Limited Companies, One Person Companies (OPC), LLPs, and Section 8 companies. Our services include name approval, drafting MOA and AOA, MCA portal filing, DIN and DSC registration, PAN/TAN applications, and post-incorporation compliance advisory.

Consumer & Labour Law

Consumer complaints, employment disputes & labour rights

How do I file a consumer complaint in India?
+
Under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, complaints can be filed before the District Commission (up to ₹1 crore), State Commission (₹1–10 crore), or National Commission (above ₹10 crore). You can also file online at edaakhil.nic.in. We assist in drafting complaints, representing you at hearings, and obtaining orders for refund, replacement, compensation, and punitive damages.
What consumer rights do I have against a builder or developer?
+
Buyers are consumers under the Consumer Protection Act and can claim relief for delayed possession, defective construction, failure to provide promised amenities, and false representations. You can approach both the Consumer Forum and MahaRERA. Courts have awarded significant compensation including interest on the purchase amount for delays.
Can you help with wrongful termination from employment?
+
Yes. Under the Industrial Disputes Act, employees in certain categories are protected against arbitrary dismissal. We handle reinstatement applications, claims for back wages, gratuity disputes, and retrenchment compensation. For managerial employees, we advise on employment contract enforcement, non-compete clauses, and POSH complaints.
What is a POSH complaint and how does the process work?
+
The Prevention of Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act, 2013 (POSH) requires employers to constitute an Internal Complaints Committee (ICC). Complaints must be filed within 3 months of the incident. We advise complainants, assist organisations in constituting ICCs and conducting inquiries, and represent parties in appeals before courts.
Can you help recover unpaid salary, gratuity or PF dues?
+
Yes. Employees are entitled to unpaid salary, gratuity (after 5 years of continuous service), earned leave encashment, and provident fund. We send legal notices, file claims before the appropriate Labour Authority, and litigate before Labour Courts or civil courts if necessary. Gratuity claims have strict timelines which we ensure are met.

IP & IT Laws

Trademarks, copyrights, patents & cybercrimes

How do I register a trademark in India?
+
Trademark registration is done through the Trade Marks Registry. The process involves trademark search, filing the application, examination by the Registry, publication in the Trade Marks Journal, a 4-month opposition period, and registration. We handle the full process and also represent clients in opposition proceedings and infringement disputes before the Bombay High Court.
What can I do if someone is copying my brand or logo?
+
If someone is using a mark identical or deceptively similar to yours, you can seek a civil injunction to immediately stop the infringement, claim damages, and in cases of deliberate copying, initiate criminal prosecution. We file urgent injunction applications, send cease and desist notices, and handle infringement suits before the Bombay High Court.
How do I protect my creative work under copyright law?
+
Copyright in India arises automatically upon creation of an original literary, artistic, musical, or cinematographic work. Formal registration with the Copyright Office is not mandatory but creates a public record and evidentiary presumption. We advise on copyright registration, licensing agreements, and represent clients in infringement disputes — particularly in content creation, software, and media.
What is a cybercrime and how can a lawyer help?
+
Cybercrimes include online fraud, identity theft, hacking, data breaches, online defamation, and cyberstalking. Under the IT Act, 2000 and IPC, these are punishable offences. We assist victims in filing complaints with the Cyber Crime Cell, filing FIRs, and pursuing civil remedies. We also defend individuals accused of cybercrimes in court.
Do you handle data privacy and IT compliance for businesses?
+
Yes. With India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDPA) in force, businesses must comply with consent management, data localisation, and breach notification requirements. We advise on privacy policies, terms of service, employee data handling, and compliance frameworks. We also represent companies in regulatory proceedings under the IT Act.

How long does a typical legal matter take?
+
Duration varies significantly. Document registrations may be completed in a few days. Consumer complaints can take 6–18 months. Civil litigation typically takes 2–5 years depending on court schedules. Criminal matters range from a few months (bail, quashing) to several years (full trial). We provide realistic timelines at your initial consultation.

+
Absolutely. We inform clients of all hearing dates, orders passed, and significant developments. You can reach us via WhatsApp at any time and request a case status update. Transparent communication is a core part of how we work with clients.
What is a legal notice and when should I send one?
+
A legal notice is a formal communication sent before initiating legal proceedings, giving the other party an opportunity to respond or settle. It is mandatory in certain matters (e.g. cheque bounce under Section 138 NI Act) and strongly advisable in most civil disputes. We draft and send notices tailored to your situation and advise on next steps based on the response.
What is the difference between a civil and criminal case?
+
A civil case involves private disputes over rights, property, contracts, or compensation — the remedy is usually monetary or injunctive. A criminal case involves the state prosecuting a person for an offence against society — the consequence can be imprisonment or fine. Some situations (cheque bounce, domestic violence, property fraud) can give rise to both civil and criminal proceedings simultaneously.
